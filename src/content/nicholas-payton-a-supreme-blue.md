A Supreme Blue pairs trumpeter and multi-instrumentalist Nicholas Payton with the Richmond, Virginia collective Butcher Brown for a reworking of two landmark recordings: Miles Davis's Kind of Blue (1959) and John Coltrane's A Love Supreme (1965). The project was released in the centennial year of both Davis and Coltrane.

The nine-track sequence follows its sources in order. The first five tracks mirror the running order of Kind of Blue — So What, Freddie Freeloader, Blue in Green, All Blues and Flamenco Sketches — while the closing four present the movements of Coltrane's A Love Supreme suite: Acknowledgement, Resolution, Pursuance and Psalm. Total running time is roughly 68 minutes.

The arrangements route the material through the band's own vocabulary, drawing on house, neo-soul, dub and hip-hop production techniques alongside jazz improvisation. Payton contributes trumpet, Rhodes, piano, vocoder, vocals and thumb piano; Butcher Brown are DJ Harrison (keyboards, piano, percussion), Corey Fonville (drums, percussion), Andrew Randazzo (bass guitar, synth bass), Marcus "Tennishu" Tenney (tenor saxophone, vocals) and Morgan Burrs (guitar, MPC).

The album was issued digitally on 24 July 2026 by Concord Jazz, with a 2LP vinyl edition following on 14 August.
