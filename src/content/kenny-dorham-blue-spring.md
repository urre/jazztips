Blue Spring is a studio album by trumpeter Kenny Dorham, recorded with alto saxophonist Cannonball Adderley and released on Riverside Records in June 1959. It was produced by Orrin Keepnews and recorded in New York City on January 20 and February 18, 1959. Original pressings credited the group as the Kenny Dorham Septet featuring Cannonball Adderley.

The album is built around a spring theme. Dorham wrote four of the six pieces ("Blue Spring", "Poetic Spring", "Spring Cannon" and "Passion Spring") and also arranged the two standards, Rodgers and Hammerstein's "It Might as Well Be Spring" and Rodgers and Hart's "Spring Is Here".

The septet has an unusual front line: Dorham's trumpet and Adderley's alto are joined by David Amram on French horn and Cecil Payne on baritone saxophone. Cedar Walton plays piano and Paul Chambers plays bass. Jimmy Cobb drums on the February session and Philly Joe Jones on the January session.
