Coltrane Jazz is a studio album by tenor saxophonist John Coltrane, released on Atlantic Records (SD 1354) in early 1961 as the follow-up to Giant Steps. It was produced by Nesuhi Ertegun and engineered by Tom Dowd at Atlantic Studios in New York City.

Most of the album was recorded across two sessions in November and December 1959, with Coltrane backed by his former Miles Davis bandmates: pianist Wynton Kelly, bassist Paul Chambers and drummer Jimmy Cobb. One track, "Village Blues", was recorded on October 21, 1960 with pianist McCoy Tyner, bassist Steve Davis and drummer Elvin Jones — an early recording of the rhythm section that would form the core of Coltrane's classic quartet.

The original LP contains eight tracks, mixing Coltrane originals such as "Fifth House", "Harmonique", "Like Sonny" and "Some Other Blues" with standards including "Little Old Lady" and "My Shining Hour". In 2000, Rhino Records reissued the album as part of its Atlantic 50th Anniversary Jazz Gallery series.
