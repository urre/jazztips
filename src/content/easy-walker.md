Easy Walker is an album by jazz saxophonist Stanley Turrentine, released in February 1968 on the Blue Note label. The album features Turrentine alongside a versatile quartet including McCoy Tyner on piano, Bob Cranshaw on bass, and Mickey Roker on drums for the initial tracks. The recording sessions took place at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, NJ, with tracks recorded across multiple dates in July 1966, July 1967, and June 1969 for bonus tracks on later reissues.

The album is known for its soulful tenor saxophone sound and engaging hard-grooving numbers, alongside sultry ballads. It includes an interpretation of Burt Bacharach and Hal David’s “What The World Needs Now Is Love” and the album opener “Meat Wave.” Critics, such as Stephen Thomas Erlewine of Allmusic, describe it as a good, relaxing soul-jazz session that allows the musicians to work a good groove and showcase their improvisational skills. The album was produced by Alfred Lion for tracks 1-7 and Francis Wolff for tracks 8-11.
